# Design decisions and change log

**Current project direction:** five-day survival; see [GDD.md](GDD.md) and CR-014. **Historical A01 baseline:** Five Nights at Bách Khoa, one-night 900-second round, solo/2 player, three Data Keys, A3/Library/B4, one Mimic FSM, baton/UV/scanner, Trust and a Blue Professor cameo. A01 is preserved under [archive](archive/A01_GDD.md). Student-team and course approval of the new direction have not been recorded.

| ID | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| D01 | Single night (900s) | Contain implementation scope |
| D02 | 1–2 players | Solo completeness + networking test |
| D03 | Baton + UV + scanner, no firearm | Evidence and defensive gameplay |
| D04 | A3 → Library → B4, then Gate 1 | Progressive tutorials/objectives |
| D05 | Innocent NPCs not defeatable; -20 Trust wrongful hit | Combat tradeoffs |
| D06 | Stylized low-poly non-graphic horror | Readable atmosphere on integrated GPU |
| D07 | Blue Professor dialogue cameo MVP, penalty minigame optional | Bounded humorous Easter egg |

### CR-014: Adopt five-day campus survival as current project direction
- Date / owner / reviewer: 2026-11-02 / user-selected project direction / student team and course review pending.
- Current baseline and AC IDs: A01 specifies a 900-second night for 1–2 players, three Data Keys, the A3–Library–B4 sequence and Gate escape; AC-01–AC-11 may be affected.
- Proposed modification: Explore a post-apocalyptic campus survival game across multiple days, ending with a distress signal and surviving until a rescue helicopter lands at a campus sports field, with a roughly 1–2-hour run, food and water that deplete moderately, moderate horror, Mimics that impersonate NPCs and player characters, light friendly-fire damage, optional hidden rooms or basements, and solo or co-op for up to four players. In solo, players can respawn at the refuge a limited number of times; in co-op, teammates can revive one another and failed revives allow limited respawns. The user selected a host-owned world saved across sessions: when the host deliberately leaves, the server attempts to save and closes the session. After an unexpected host disconnect, wait 60 real seconds for their return while game time and dangerous activity are paused and gameplay actions, including movement and inventory changes, are locked. Remaining players see a waiting-for-host message and the 60-second countdown. During helicopter boarding, pause the departure countdown and any still-valid vote countdown; if the host was aboard, remove them from the roster and cancel an active vote. The rule canceling a vote on a boarding-roster change still applies. A save made while the helicopter waits restores the helicopter and remaining boarding time on resume; the remaining boarding countdown starts when the host enters the resumed run. Players recorded aboard at the successful save are restored aboard if they rejoin before departure; those who have not rejoined at departure receive Disconnected, not Rescued. Do not restore an active departure vote; a new vote can be started. The saved roster includes any removals before the save. If they return within that window, resume immediately from the paused state; otherwise attempt to save and close, including during boarding. On failure the group is informed and can resume only from the most recent successful save. A living non-host who disconnects safely disappears from the world and reappears at the active base on reconnect to the running session. Their carried items remain in inventory, inaccessible to teammates merely because of disconnection. If downed on disconnect, their character remains visible for teammates to revive, the 30-second timer continues while the session runs, and normal revive, respawn or elimination rules apply. If they reconnect while still downed, they remain at the downed location with the remaining timer. If revived while disconnected, the group is notified, their character safely disappears and they reappear at the active base upon reconnecting. If eliminated while disconnected after exhausting respawns, they enter spectator mode on reconnect while the run continues. Food and water do not decrease and zero-need damage pauses while a non-host is disconnected; the damage resumes on reconnect if either need remains zero. If they automatically respawn while disconnected, their 5-second respawn immunity starts on reconnect and ends earlier if they attack; a teammate revive does not grant that immunity; respawn still drops the items in a bag accessible to teammates. On reconnect during the active session, restore the server-recorded disconnect state as updated by those events, including inventory and remaining respawns, reconciled with later valid transfers; an item transferred to another player is not recreated. Each item has one owner or location. After session closure, the last successful save is the recovery point and unsaved changes may be lost. Pause implementation and delivery to a disconnected host remain to be designed. The user also selected a university behavioral-simulation experiment that created the Mimics and caused the disaster. See [GDD_SURVIVAL_DRAFT.md](GDD_SURVIVAL_DRAFT.md).
- Refuge direction: Players may choose any campus building as a base and later move to another, transporting their supplies. Each building has predefined power, storage and defense upgrades that fit its real layout and use. Fixed upgrades remain at the old building and can be used again when the group returns; access to supplies left behind remains open. Initially, Mimics were selected to attack the base only at night, not every night; the later selected post-night-5 grace-period waves extend the attack window; danger escalates and players receive advance signs. They can break reinforced doors and defensive equipment, which players can repair; exact attack frequency and detailed damage rules remain open. Any first prototype with fewer buildings is only an incremental validation step, not the final scope.
- Crafting direction: Keep a small number of clear recipes for essential supplies and defensive repairs. Specific recipes, ingredients and workstation rules remain open.
- Needs and inventory: Food and water each start at 100 and decrease by 1 and 1.5 per real minute respectively; reaching 0 on either or both causes 1 HP total loss every 5 seconds. A food portion restores 35 food, a water bottle restores 40 water, and a medkit restores 40 HP; each meter caps at 100. Inventory has 8 slots with up to 5 items of the same type per slot. If no inventory capacity is available for a pickup, the item stays in place and the player receives an inventory-full message. Food, water and ammunition are replenished in limited quantities by in-game day; total supplies increase with party size while encouraging sharing. Replenishment starts at the beginning of day 2 and occurs at the start of each new in-game day at suitable locations that vary by day; day 1 uses initially placed supplies. Supply points are not marked on the map and players discover them. Uncollected supplies remain and each location has a shared cap of 10 items across all types, counting each food portion, water bottle or ammunition box as one item. Replenished supplies are shared and any player can pick up and share them. The server grants a simultaneously requested item to only one player and informs the other that it was taken. When selected points lack capacity, prioritize water, then food, then ammunition; alternate among the three ammunition types if not all boxes fit. Starting on day 2, rotate the starting type on successive replenishment days pistol → shotgun → rifle, then repeat. Skip a type once its daily allocation is filled and continue with types still owed; the rotation and processed-day state persist across save and resume without granting a day’s replenishment twice. The game clock and replenishment pause while the saved run is closed, and resume from the last successful save. Discard unplaced replenishment for that day without carrying it over. The number of connected players at the start of the day determines replenishment quantity; a later join changes the allocation only at the next daily replenishment. Solo allocation is 3 food, 4 water and 4 ammunition boxes (2 pistol, 1 shotgun, 1 rifle); each additional player increases each solo quantity by 50%, rounded up separately. For 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 connected players this gives food 3 / 5 / 6 / 8, water 4 / 6 / 8 / 10, and boxes 4 / 6 / 8 / 10, subject to point capacity. Pistol/shotgun/rifle box allocations are 3/2/1, 4/2/2 and 5/3/2 for 2, 3 and 4 connected players respectively. Selected points may include the active base, but at most floor(25% of the total number of items actually replenished that day) appear there, with supplies also distributed in other areas. Exact locations remain open.
- Threat direction: Include mutated creatures and machines from university experiments in addition to Mimics. Players identify Mimics through observable anomalies and can optionally verify with UV or a scanner. Machines patrol and detect players using light or sound; mutated creatures primarily hide, listen for noise and ambush. The user wants dense, horde-like base waves, with solo totals of 10, 14, 18 and 24 attackers per wave on nights 2–5. Each additional player adds 50% of the solo count, rounded up; active wave-enemy caps are 20, 30, 40 and 50 for 1–4 players. Each wave uses the party size recorded at its start; joins or departures change the next wave only. Nights keep their selected durations; earlier groups retreat on a schedule to free capacity for remaining wave spawns rather than extending the night. Exact timing, eligible player states and stuck-enemy recovery remain open; the large counts need balance and performance profiling. Exact sensor rules, cues and counterplay remain open, as do creature identities and the threats' specific origins. A Mimic-only first playable slice would be an incremental test, not the final threat roster.
- Wave snapshot: At wave start, count connected players who are alive or downed; do not count a player who exhausted respawns and is spectating. Changes after the snapshot affect the next wave. Waiting-to-respawn and no-eligible-player edge cases remain open.
- Wave composition and weapons: Base waves consist mainly of mutated creatures, with occasional experimental machines and Mimics. Players begin with a melee weapon and find a pistol, shotgun, rifle and limited ammunition in the school; their loaded capacities are 12, 6 and 30 rounds respectively. Each firearm uses its own box type, with full boxes holding 20, 10 and 30 rounds respectively; each slot holds up to five boxes of the same type, and partly used boxes retain remaining rounds. Pistol, shotgun and rifle base enemy damage is 25, up to 60 for a full close-range shotgun hit, and 35 HP per shot respectively; minimum intervals are 0.4, 1.2 and 0.15 seconds respectively. Reload times are 2 seconds, 0.7 seconds per shell and 2.8 seconds respectively; shotgun reloading can be interrupted to fire. A firearm shot against a teammate deals 5 HP and can down them; a shotgun applies this at most once per shot to the same teammate. Exact wave proportions, ammunition supply, shotgun spread remains open; the A01 prototype did not include guns.
- Firing modes and noise: A shotgun shot has 6 pellets of up to 10 HP each. If all hit an enemy, damage is 60 HP through 15 studs and decreases linearly to 30 HP at 40 studs and 0 HP at 65 studs. Friendly fire remains 5 HP at most per shot per teammate. The rifle fires automatically while the fire input is held; the pistol and shotgun fire once per press. Gunshots attract enemies within 60, 100 and 120 studs for the pistol, shotgun and rifle respectively; walls do not reduce hearing range. Enemy sound prioritization remains open, and gunshot attraction does not create daytime base-attack waves.
- Rescue outcome: A helicopter lands at a campus sports field. Each player aboard at departure is rescued; the helicopter departs automatically after at most 3 minutes, or earlier if a strict majority of players aboard agrees. All connected players see the boarding countdown. A downed player cannot be carried aboard and must be revived before boarding; a player downed outside at departure is Left Behind. Players aboard the landed helicopter are immune to all damage, including hunger and thirst damage, and may leave before departure; if a player is nevertheless downed while already aboard, they are rescued if still aboard at departure. A player disconnected at departure receives Disconnected and is not rescued; a connected eliminated spectator receives Eliminated and is not rescued. Before a departure vote starts, show the initiating boarded player a warning listing all group members not aboard, including downed, eliminated and disconnected statuses; update it if the boarding roster changes while open, and let them choose whether to proceed. If the initiator leaves the helicopter, close the warning and require boarding again before initiating; the three-minute deadline still triggers immediate departure while the warning is open. Any boarded player can initiate a departure vote and automatically casts one yes vote; players aboard may change or withdraw their votes until departure or the vote ends. The vote lasts at most 10 seconds; changes to the boarded roster cancel it, and a new vote can be initiated. A boarded player who disconnects before departure is removed from the boarded and voting rosters, canceling an active vote; on reconnect before departure they appear at the active base under the normal living non-host rule. The helicopter departs immediately upon a strict majority; with one player aboard, their automatic yes on initiating the vote causes immediate departure. If it departs with nobody aboard, the group loses. If the vote expires without one, it waits for another vote; it departs immediately at the three-minute boarding timeout even if a vote is underway. Other vote interaction details remain open.
- Story and rescue: The university experiment originally modeled disaster response and evacuation; a technical failure caused its simulation to develop beyond expectations and create the Mimics. The distress signal is received by a rescue group organized by survivors outside the campus quarantine zone, which sends the helicopter. The failure's precise mechanism and the rescue group's members and resources remain open.
- Survivor NPCs and Trust: Survivors provide information, supplies and optional side quests, and do not follow the group. Shared group Trust starts at 100, stays within 0–100 and decreases by 10 per validated hit or shot against innocent NPCs; one shot hitting multiple innocent NPCs incurs only one −10 penalty. Each valid act of helping or completed side quest restores 10, up to 100; each side quest awards Trust only once per run, and repeatable help awards it at most once per NPC per in-game day for the group. Depending on the NPC, help can involve delivering supplies, repair or clearing an obstacle. At least one NPC in the school offers a repeatable help task requiring no supplies each in-game day, subject to the same per-NPC daily limit. NPC responses are normal at 70–100, offer 50% of normal supplies rounded down with a minimum of one if stock exists at 30–69, and no trading with essential communication only at 0–29; rescue information and eligible Trust recovery remain available subject to daily limits. An NPC may defend themselves when directly attacked and stops after the attacker stops attacking and stays more than 20 studs away continuously for 10 seconds. A survivor NPC has 100 maximum HP. At zero HP an innocent NPC is incapacitated, recovers automatically after 60 seconds with 50 HP and does not die permanently. Exact helping-action eligibility and handling multiple or disconnected attackers remain open.
- Objective locations: The user selected random locations between runs for restoring power, finding the frequency and obtaining transmitter components, using buildings appropriate to each task. Exact objective positions are shown on the map and tasks can be completed in any order. Candidate pools and selection details remain to be designed; assigned locations must be preserved for the same saved run.
- Recovery and friendly fire: Each player has three respawns per run. A validated attack on a teammate deals 5 HP and may down them at zero HP. In co-op, teammates have a 30-second window to complete a 5-second hold, restoring 40 HP; an attack on the rescuer or leaving interaction range interrupts and resets the hold; a revive retains the player’s food and water; if either is zero, the selected 1 HP per 5 seconds total damage continues. At expiry a player with a respawn remaining immediately respawns at the active base with 100 HP and food and water set to exactly 50 each regardless of previous values, and consumes one use. On respawn they are immune to damage for 5 seconds or until they attack, whichever comes first. On respawn the player's carried items drop as a bag at the downed location; its owner and teammates may take items. A co-op player with no respawns remaining can still be revived within the 30-second window; if it expires they spectate. If everyone is downed but someone has a respawn remaining, wait for automatic respawn. If everyone is downed or eliminated with no respawns remaining, the group loses; playing again starts a new run. Interaction range, safeguards against repeated harassment, initial-spawn fallback, bag recovery fallback and disconnected-player treatment remain subject to review.
- Naming and rescue pacing: The user selected a five-day structure with 12-minute days, selected Five Nights at Bách Khoa (FNABK) as the GDD title and English as the submission language, and chose helicopter arrival approximately 4 minutes after both signaling and night 5. Signaling is available as soon as power is restored and the frequency and transmitter components are obtained. If the group has not sent the signal by the end of night 5, it has 10 real minutes after night 5 to do so or lose. Up to three base attack waves start at minutes 1, 4 and 7 after night 5 during this grace period, with 24 total attackers solo per wave (20 creatures, 3 machines and 1 Mimic) and the selected party scaling (36, 48 and 60 total for 2, 3 and 4 players). Co-op composition stays close to the solo proportions and is rounded to the total. The selected compositions are 30/5/1, 40/6/2 and 50/8/2 creatures/machines/Mimics for 2, 3 and 4 players. Each grace-period wave has a sound and text warning 30 seconds before its start. If a signal is sent after the warning but before the wave starts, cancel the wave and notify players. Remaining grace-period attackers start retreating at 3:30, 6:30 and 9:30 after night 5 respectively; the first two leave before the next wave spawns; stuck attackers are removed outside player view when possible; any still visible at the scheduled start disappear with an effect before the new wave starts on time. Sending the signal stops new grace-period waves; an active wave continues on its original retreat schedule and attackers already retreating continue their retreat. If the signal was sent before night 5 ends, no new base wave starts during the helicopter wait, while already active enemies remain dangerous. Spawn pacing and retreat completion remain under review. Nightly attack wave counts are 0, 2, 2, 3 and 4, totaling 11, and night lengths are 5, 5, 5, 6 and 8 real minutes. Attacker counts increase with party size while remaining defensible solo. Exact counts, enemy composition and wave timing remain open.
- Consolidated design: [GDD.md](GDD.md) is the current user-selected project direction, and marks proposed rules, values, scope and timing for review. [GDD_SURVIVAL_COMPLETE.md](GDD_SURVIVAL_COMPLETE.md) is a historical pre-migration snapshot; [A01_GDD.md](archive/A01_GDD.md) preserves the previous A01 GDD.
- Rationale and player experience impact: Use the large campus for purposeful expeditions, preparation and optional discovery. Exact survival rules, escape conditions and session structure remain open.
- Development effort / risks: This changes the project design and expands multiplayer scope, content, networking, testing and performance requirements. The detached campus preview is not verified as integrated gameplay. Current code still implements the A01 prototype; [new acceptance criteria](ACCEPTANCE.md) are all untested. Previous A01 design and evidence are preserved, not deleted.
- Validation evidence: Concept discussion and documentation only; no new gameplay tests have run.
- Approval decision: The user selected the overall direction and requested that it become the current repository design on 2026-11-02. Specific mechanics and student-team/course approval remain pending; no approval by others is implied.
